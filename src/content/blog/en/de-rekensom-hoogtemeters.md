Behind the impressive 17,500 vertical meters hides a painful calculation. Spread over ten days without rest days, that comes down to an average of 1,750 vertical meters per day. To put that in perspective: every single day that is a gruelling climb that would already strike fear into many a trained cyclist. It is the ultimate physical translation of the extreme challenge that 15/70 brings.

The route around Albir and the Costa Blanca is not chosen at random. This region has long, treacherous climbs, sharp hairpin bends and sometimes scorching heat. Add the unpredictable headwind attack zone in the inland area and you understand why this project within the FORZA FORTUNA Financial Group team is called the 'extremum'.

It is the absolute point where experience, character, passion and physical exhaustion meet to prove that with the right mindset anything is achievable.

Yet it is not just blindly looking at numbers. Terro's preparation is thoughtful and meticulously structured: from core training and intensive strength work in winter to the specific stage training in Spain itself.

With that professional approach, 17,500 is no longer a random figure, but an ambitious and achievable goal on the way to the autumn of 2029.
